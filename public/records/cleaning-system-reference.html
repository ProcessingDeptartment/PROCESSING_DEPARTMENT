This page is the rationale record for the REC 7.6.x restructure. It is reference material, not a controlled record — but the decisions on it are the ones that need sign-off from the document owner before the new forms are issued. The live item master is on the Master Cleaning Plan page.

1 · What was there before

Eleven documents governed cleaning. Read end to end, they contained three genuinely different jobs mixed together across ten forms:

  1. Cleaning was done — the cleaner's record (REC 7.6.0 a/b/c)
  2. Cleaning was inspected — QC's visual verification (REC 7.6.1, 7.6.1.1, 7.6.2, 7.6.2.2)
  3. Cleaning was validated — objective evidence, i.e. a swab (REC 7.6.8)

Two document-control findings, before any redesign.
  • REC 7.6.2 had two live copies on the desktop — one headed Revision 2 (15/04/2020), one filed as "rev3" whose header actually says Revision 4 (22/09/2022). The Master Index List (REC 01 Rev 16) says Revision 4, 22/09/2022, so the index is the tie-breaker and the Rev 2 copy is obsolete. The digitised record is set to baseline Rev 4.
  • REC 7.2.5.1 Master Cleaning Checklist is a feed-mill document. Its content is extruder, pre-conditioner, pellet spreader, vacuum coater, bagging line, Phase 1/2/3 and the Starter Feed mezzanine — a different site and scope from abalone processing. It has been left out of the cleaning item master entirely and keeps its own separate record page. Mixing it into the processing set is what an auditor would call a scope error.

Roughly 110 of REC 7.6.1's 175 rows were the same twelve generic checks repeated per area, and about 100 of REC 7.6.1.1's rows repeated those same lines again in the same week, with REC 7.6.2 asking for a third pass at a coarser grain. One drain in the scrubbing area could be ticked on REC 7.6.0 a, REC 7.6.1, REC 7.6.1.1 and REC 7.6.2 in a single week — four forms, four signatures, one drain.

On top of that: frequency was declared in the Master Cleaning Plan, again in REC 7.2.5.1's Frequency column, and again implied by which form the inspector was holding; REC 7.6.1 and REC 7.6.1.1 carried the identical Cleaning Instruction paragraph word for word; and the dispatch area appeared on REC 7.6.2.2, in REC 7.6.1's FG02 block and on REC 7.6.0 a.

The digitised versions were worse than the paper. Before this change, REC 7.6.1 was built on form-record.js with a free-text roster: Area and Item were blank text boxes the inspector typed themselves. All 175 enumerated line items had been lost — so the digital record could not prove which items were inspected, and two inspectors would produce non-comparable records. REC 7.6.2.2 was on monitoring-log.js, a periodic numeric-reading engine, which has no place to record a corrective action.

3 · What was built

One item master, one engine, three record families. Every REC 7.6.x page is now a filtered view over the same data — no document number was retired.

3.1 The item master — lib/cleaning-master-data.js

Every cleanable item in the facility, stated once, with its frequency, responsible role, method, chemical, whether it is a food contact surface, whether it needs a swab, and which records it appears on. Transcribed from the Master Cleaning Plan Rev 2 merged with every line item in REC 7.6.0 a/b/c, 7.6.1, 7.6.1.1, 7.6.2 and 7.6.2.2. Browse it on the Master Cleaning Plan page.

3.2 The twelve standard checks

The generic checks that repeated in nearly every area block are declared once:

  1. Floors clean, no standing water
  2. Drains clean and free-flowing
  3. Walls clean, no flaking paint or rust
  4. Doors, door seals and roller doors clean and closed
  5. Ceiling clean
  6. Light covers and overhead structures clean and intact
  7. Windows and window seals clean, free from dust
  8. Ventilation / extractor fans clean and working
  9. Hand wash basin clean, warm water and paper towel available
  10. Soap and sanitiser dispenser clean and full
  11. Cleaning equipment clean, stored correctly, facing away from walls
  12. Waste bin emptied and clean

On a record they collapse into a single per-area control — "Area conforms? C / NC / N/A" — together with that area's general housekeeping items. Marking an area NC expands all of them so the failing one can be named, with its finding, correction, corrective-action due date and CAR reference. Food contact surfaces are excluded from the roll-up and always shown (see §5).

3.3 The engine — lib/cleaning-register.js

This is the system's third record shape. monitoring-log.js handles periodic date/shift numeric logs; form-record.js handles single-event structured forms. A cleaning checklist against a controlled item master, with exception drill-down and two-level sign-off, is neither — which is exactly why forcing it into form-record.js had destroyed the line-item detail.

3.4 Families — how the double entry actually disappears

Records that were filled in the same period by the same people now open the same submission. family decides that; scope and zones decide which slice of it a page renders.

What rolls up, and what never does

Each area gets one control covering the 12 standard checks plus that area's general housekeeping items ("no bins on the floor", "board clean"). They expand only if the area is marked NC.

Food contact surfaces never roll up. All 17 of them are ticked individually on every run, no matter how clean the shift was — those are the items an auditor wants individual attention on, and 12 of them also render a swab / ATP result field. That split is why the daily inspection is 45 controls rather than 33: the extra 12 are deliberate.

Deliberately not built: a "mark all pass" button. It would have taken the daily inspection to one tap, but a single control that passes an entire plant is a rubber stamp, and an auditor reads it that way. One control per area is the level at which someone has actually looked.

Sign-off model

The paper forms used four inconsistent role sets — REC 7.6.0 had doer + supervisor but no QC; REC 7.6.1 had inspector + verifier but no record of who actually cleaned; REC 7.6.2 had checker + supervisor. All records now use the same two levels, and the engine refuses a verification signed by the same person who performed the check. It also refuses to verify a record that still has a non-conformance with no correction recorded.

These guards cannot run yet. Login is disabled system-wide, so no signature can be attributed to a person at all — see gap 8. The Sign and Verify buttons are disabled with a visible reason until login is re-enabled.

6 · Why the collapsed form is still auditable

The obvious objection to hiding twelve checks behind one control is "how do you prove they were checked". Three things answer it:

  1. The standard-check list lives in the controlled item master, not in the UI, and carries its own version number (standardChecksVersion).
  2. That version is stamped onto every saved submission, along with the item master version. A record from any date can be replayed against exactly the check list that was in force that day, even after the list changes.
  3. Marking an area NC forces the specific failing check to be named — the exception path captures more detail than the paper form's single "Non-conformance" column did.

Changing the item master changes every record that renders it, which is precisely why a change there requires a document revision bump through document-revision.js with a reason, a name and a title — the same rule as every other record in this system.

7 · Open gaps — these are FSSC findings, not design choices

Compressing the paperwork does not close these. They were missing from the paper system and are still missing; the new structure just makes them visible instead of hiding them across ten forms. The Master Cleaning Plan page computes the first two live from the master.

#GapWhy it matters
1No chemical, concentration or contact time. The Master Cleaning Plan names only "soap" and "dynacide"; no concentration or contact time appears on any document.ISO/TS 22002-1 §11.2 expects cleaning agents and their conditions of use to be documented. Left blank in the master rather than guessed — an invented concentration on a controlled record is worse than a blank one.
2No sanitising confirmation. REC 7.6.1's own instruction demands end-of-shift sanitising of work surfaces; no form has a field for it.An instruction with no record is an unverifiable control.
3No changeover cleaning record. "Between different jobs" is instructed but never captured in processing.This is the allergen and species cross-contamination exposure. Items with frequency "Every changeover" are flagged in the master.
4Effectiveness verification is visual only, except allergen swabs.FSSC wants evidence that cleaning works. Food contact items are tagged SWAB in the master and the engine renders a swab / ATP result field on them — but a swab schedule still has to be set.
5No cleaning equipment colour-coding recorded. "Stored correctly" is inspected; the colour system itself is not documented.Segregation of cleaning equipment between raw, live and high-care zones.
6Master Cleaning Plan has no document number — it is literally "1" — and 3 of its 18 rows have a blank WHO.Document control. An unnumbered controlled document cannot be indexed or superseded.
7No time stamp on cleaning. Start of shift vs. between jobs vs. end of shift is indistinguishable.The engine records save timestamps, but the stage of the shift is still not a field.
8No signature can be attributed to anyone — system-wide. Login is switched off in lib/login-ui.js (ensureAuthenticated() resolves immediately without prompting), so Auth.createSignOff() returns null and no record in the system can capture who signed it.Found while testing this build, and it affects every record, not just cleaning. The cleaning register now disables its Sign and Verify buttons and says why, rather than reporting a signature that was never stored. Drafts still save. Until login is re-enabled, no cleaning record can meet the FSSC requirement for identified, independent verification.
9No product / batch link. REC 7.6.8 records the product before and after an allergen wash-down as free text.The system already has job-number tracing (traceability.js, batch_link:*). Wiring REC 7.6.8's before/after products to it is a small change with real recall value.

8 · Before go-live — decisions and actions for the document owner

Document revisions have deliberately NOT been bumped. Each rewired record still carries its paper baseline revision. A revision bump requires a reason, a name and a title from the person making it, and the system records who did it — so it must be done by the document owner through the app, not fabricated here. Every one of these forms changed materially and needs a bump before it is issued.
  1. Bump the revision on REC 7.6.0 a/b/c, 7.6.1, 7.6.1.1, 7.6.2 and 7.6.2.2, with the reason "restructured onto shared cleaning item master; standard checks consolidated".
  2. Retrieve the obsolete REC 7.6.2 Rev 2 paper copy — Rev 4 is the current one per the Master Index List.
  3. Confirm REC 7.2.5.1 belongs to the feed mill and move it out of the processing document set.
  4. Give the Master Cleaning Plan a real document number and add it to the Master Index List.
  5. Fill in the chemical, concentration and contact time for every food contact item — the gap list on the Master Cleaning Plan page is the worklist.
  6. Set a swab / ATP schedule for the items already tagged for it.
  7. Verify the item master against the floor. It was transcribed from the documents, not walked. Any area or item that no longer exists — or exists and was never on a form — must be corrected before issue.
